# Play the narration track in the Android app through the WebView with a Media3 media session

[ADR 0011](0011-serve-one-narration-track-and-play-it-with-one-audio-element.md) left the Android and iOS shells playing the narration track through the web player's one `<audio>` in their WebView, best effort, and the system media controls at browser defaults. On Android that failed the listener twice. Android treats Cup as a cached background app: about 5 to 7 seconds after Cup leaves the foreground its requests to `cup.audio` fail, so listening-position saves fail, and 60 to 90 seconds later the cached apps freezer stops the audio ([research](../research/android-background-network-access.md), 2026-11-14). And Android WebView disables the Media Session API, so Cup had no media notification, nothing on the lock screen, and earbuds, headsets and a headphone disconnect did not reach it. The map [#371](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/371) (Linear [CUP-41](https://linear.app/cup-audio/issue/CUP-41)) and its spec [#392](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/392) decided how to fix that on 2026-11-16.

## Decision

- **The WebView's `<audio>` stays the only engine** ([#376](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/376)). Cup's Android app gets no native player and keeps ADR 0011's one element. A `mediaPlayback` foreground service keeps Cup unfrozen and its network reachable while it plays ([#373](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/373) showed that on the emulator), which removed the main reason to go native.
- **One own, app-local Java Capacitor plugin wraps Media3's `MediaSessionService`** ([#376](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/376)). It lives in the Android shell's app module and is registered in `MainActivity`. iOS keeps WebKit's own Now Playing and gets no plugin.
- **One command path on every platform** ([#388](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/388)). The web app's media session module is a port with two implementations, chosen by platform: over `navigator.mediaSession` in browsers and iOS, and over the plugin on Android. Both call the same handlers, which act like Cup's own controls: `play` and `pause` through the player host's Play and Pause, `stop` as pause, `seekto`, and skips by the system's offset or 15 s. The plugin sends every system command, from the notification, the lock screen, headset buttons, media keys and earbuds, to the page as one `command` event, and never touches the `<audio>`.
- **The plugin's player only mirrors the page** ([#388](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/388)). A Media3 `SimpleBasePlayer` holds one media item with the title and the cover image's signed URL, and the state the page last reported: `playWhenReady` from playing, ready while loaded, buffering between `waiting` and `playing`, ended after `ended`, and a position that Media3 extrapolates at the reported rate. It advertises play/pause, stop, seek in the current item, seek back, seek forward and the metadata getters; stop must be advertised, or Media3 drops it from car head units, Android Auto and Wear OS. The notification shows back 15 s, play/pause and forward 15 s, and no stop button.
- **The service lifecycle** ([#389](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/389)). It starts on the first `playing` of a loaded audiobook, always after a tap in Cup's visible UI, so Android 12's ban on starting foreground services from the background never applies; opening an audiobook or loading it to seek starts nothing. After a pause, the end included, it keeps Media3's 10-minute foreground grace, then leaves the foreground and keeps the notification. A Play from outside Cup after the grace brings it back to the foreground before the plugin sends `play` to the page, because on Android 15 and later WebView's audio focus request, and with it Chromium's `play()`, fails otherwise. It stops in exactly three cases: the page clears the port (a change of the signed-in user, or `release` of a deleted audiobook), Cup's activity is destroyed, or the listener swipes the notification away after the grace. The manifest declares `FOREGROUND_SERVICE` and `FOREGROUND_SERVICE_MEDIA_PLAYBACK`, and no `POST_NOTIFICATIONS`, since media session notifications are exempt; there is no `onPlaybackResumption` and no `MediaButtonReceiver`, so a media button after the service stopped does not relaunch Cup.
- **Headphones and audio focus** ([#390](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/390)). The plugin registers an `ACTION_AUDIO_BECOMING_NOISY` receiver while the session exists and sends `pause` down the command path; nothing resumes on reconnect. Earbud Pause and Play arrive as media session commands, so Cup has no in-ear logic. Audio focus stays with WebView, which pauses for a call and resumes after it, pauses for good when another app plays, and ducks to 20 % for a prompt; the page reports these as ordinary playback events, so the plugin requests and handles no focus.

## Considered options

- **A native player** (a hand-written ExoPlayer, or Capawesome's Audio Player). Its gain is playback and commands that do not depend on the page's JavaScript running, plus built-in noisy and focus handling. Rejected: a second engine behind the web player's timeline and `PlaybackSession`, a lasting cost on every player change, while position saves would still stall whenever the page is frozen ([#376](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/376), reconsidered in [#388](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/388)).
- **Capawesome's Media Session plugin.** Rejected with its Audio Player: paid and closed source, so its manifest cannot be audited before buying, and it ties Cup to a subscription.
- **`@capgo/capacitor-media-session`.** Rejected: it lacks `FOREGROUND_SERVICE_MEDIA_PLAYBACK`, handles neither noisy nor focus, which would split Cup's native code from the session, and writes `MPNowPlayingInfoCenter` on iOS.
- **`@jofr/capacitor-media-session`.** Rejected: built for Capacitor 6 and unmaintained.
- **A `navigator.mediaSession` polyfill on Android.** Rejected in favour of the port, which keeps the two implementations explicit ([#388](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/388)).
- **Stopping the service at once on a pause in the background,** which makes a later Play from the notification impossible, and **staying in the foreground indefinitely while paused,** which Media3 discourages ([#389](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/389)).
- **Cup's own audio focus handling.** Rejected: WebView already holds focus, and a second owner would fight it ([#390](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/390)).

## Relationship to existing decisions

This supersedes two bullets of [ADR 0011](0011-serve-one-narration-track-and-play-it-with-one-audio-element.md): "Native players removed … best effort" for Android, which now plays through the WebView with this media session (iOS stays best effort until the map [#386](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/386)), and "System media controls use browser defaults", which the media session port replaces on every platform. Everything else in ADR 0011 stands, the one `<audio>` element included.

## Consequences

- With the service in the foreground, Cup is neither frozen nor cut off from the network, so playback and position saves continue with the screen locked or in another app. After the grace Android may freeze Cup again; a save that fails then is saved again when Cup returns to the foreground ([CUP-71](https://linear.app/cup-audio/issue/CUP-71)).
- A Play from the notification or earbuds works only while the page can run JavaScript. **Unverified:** whether a page Android froze after the grace receives a forwarded Play and plays, including a track URL renewal, and whether the Android 15 ordering holds on a real device. The frozen-page probe [#397](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/397) settles this; evidence of lost commands reopens [#376](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/376).
- A call longer than the grace may need a manual Play afterwards; that is accepted.
- The mobile E2E suite checks the session, the foreground service and a media key reaching the page on the emulator, but loopback is exempt from the network cut, so locked listening is checked by hand on a physical device.

### Implementation status

The plugin with its start, mirror and command path was built for [#396](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/396) on the spec's integration branch, 2026-11-16. The stop cases ([#398](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/398)) and the noisy receiver ([#399](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/399)) are separate tickets of the spec, not yet built. The [mobile app guide](../../apps/mobile-app/README.md#playback) describes the plugin, and the [conversion architecture](../architecture/conversion.md#playback) the port.
