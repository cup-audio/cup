# Play the narration track in the Android app with a native Media3 ExoPlayer

> Note, 2026-11-17: the manual Pixel protocol below was not checked in. The operator ran the Pixel check once, by hand, as CUP-41's acceptance check, and every item passed ([#435](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/435#issuecomment-6943452049)); the operator then dropped the checked-in protocol. No checklist lives in the repository, so the rerun trigger in Consequences (the plugin's native code, its Media3 version or the target Android version changes) names no artifact to rerun.

[ADR 0011](0011-serve-one-narration-track-and-play-it-with-one-audio-element.md) left the Android and iOS shells playing the narration track through the web player's one `<audio>` in their WebView, best effort, and the system media controls at browser defaults. On Android that failed the listener twice. Android treats Cup as a cached background app: about 5 to 7 seconds after Cup leaves the foreground its requests to `cup.audio` fail, so listening-position saves fail, and 60 to 90 seconds later the cached apps freezer stops the audio ([research](../research/android-background-network-access.md), 2026-11-14). And Android WebView disables the Media Session API, so Cup had no media notification, nothing on the lock screen, and earbuds, headsets and a headphone disconnect did not reach it.

A first decision kept the WebView's `<audio>` as the engine and mirrored it in a Media3 session (built on the integration branch for [#396](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/396), commit `8e3fbcf9`). The frozen-page probe on the operator's Pixel 10 Pro (Android 17, WebView 157) then showed that a Play from the earbuds after a pause reaches the session, but the WebView has suspended the hidden page's JavaScript by then, so the command never reaches the `<audio>` and nothing plays ([#397](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/397)). The engine was decided again on 2026-11-16 in [#376](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/376), and the map [#371](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/371) (Linear [CUP-41](https://linear.app/cup-audio/issue/CUP-41)) and its revised spec [#392](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/392) follow it.

## Decision

- **ExoPlayer is the engine on Android** ([#376](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/376)). Cup's own app-local Java Capacitor plugin, registered in `MainActivity`, holds a Media3 ExoPlayer inside its `MediaPlaybackService`, a `MediaSessionService`. ExoPlayer is the only source of playback state on Android; the page creates no `<audio>` there. Browsers keep ADR 0011's `<audio>`, and iOS keeps the WebView `<audio>`, best effort, until the map [#386](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/386) decides otherwise.
- **The page drives it through the player port** ([#423](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/423)). `PlayerHostServices.createPlayer` builds each loaded audiobook's `Player`: on an `<audio>` in browsers and iOS, on the plugin on Android, chosen once where the services are built. `PlayerHost` keeps deciding what loads, when the track URL renews and when to unload. The native-backed player requires the account's `subject`, because only signed-in listeners play in the app ([ADR 0017](0017-keep-trial-links-out-of-the-mobile-apps.md)).
  - **Commands:** `load` sets the media item without preparing it; `play` prepares and plays; `pause`, `seekTo` and `skipBy`, which skips from ExoPlayer's own position; `replaceUrls` keeps the position; `close("save" | "discard")` releases the player and stops the service; `getState`.
  - **State:** one `state` event after every change of ExoPlayer, with no periodic ticks: the conversion and subject, playing, buffering, ended, the duration, the position with the time it was read, from which the page extrapolates, and an error. The page also calls `getState` whenever it becomes visible, because events sent while the WebView suspends the hidden page can be lost.
- **System controls act on the player directly** ([#376](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/376)). The notification, the lock screen, media keys, headsets, earbuds, car head units and Wear OS reach ExoPlayer's session without the page. The media item carries the title and the cover's signed URL as artwork. The notification shows back 15 s, play/pause and forward 15 s, with no stop button; stop stays advertised, or Media3 drops it from car head units, Android Auto and Wear OS, and Cup handles it as pause. Tapping the notification opens Cup.
- **Audio focus and headphone disconnects** ([#390](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/390), as amended by [#376](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/376)). ExoPlayer handles focus with speech audio attributes and pauses when headphones disconnect; the plugin registers no receiver of its own. A transient loss, such as a call, pauses and resumes. A duck request, such as a navigation prompt, pauses and resumes instead of lowering the volume. A permanent loss, such as another app's media, pauses for good. A disconnect never resumes on reconnect.
- **The service lifecycle** ([#389](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/389), as amended). The service starts on the first `play`, always a tap in Cup's visible UI, so Android 12's ban on starting foreground services from the background never applies; `load` and seeks before Play start nothing and show no notification. After a pause, the end included, it keeps Media3's foreground grace, then leaves the foreground and keeps its notification. It stops when the page closes it (sign-out, a change of the signed-in user, deleting the loaded audiobook, or a started page that cannot adopt the session), when the listener swipes a paused Cup from recents or the notification away after the grace, and when a paused session's URLs expire or the track answers `403`. The manifest declares `FOREGROUND_SERVICE` and `FOREGROUND_SERVICE_MEDIA_PLAYBACK`, and no `POST_NOTIFICATIONS`, since media session notifications are exempt; there is no `onPlaybackResumption` and no `MediaButtonReceiver`, so a media button after the service stopped does not relaunch Cup.
- **Errors and renewal.** Only the page renews the signed track URL, on its own Plays, as in the browser, and after an expiry. When ExoPlayer gives up loading the track, as in a dead zone, the plugin pauses and the page shows "Audio could not be played. Press Play to try again."; Play prepares the item again at the same position.
- **Native listening-position saves** ([#424](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/424), [#425](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/425)). On Android the plugin is the only writer of the listening position, by the web player's rules including the CUP-71 retry, through a signed position URL that the server issues next to the signed track URL ([authentication](../architecture/authentication.md#signed-position-url)). It needs no Supabase session, so it works while the page is suspended, and a final save on sign-out cannot write into another account.
- **Testing** ([#426](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/426)). Everything the emulator can show runs in the Android mobile E2E suite against the test Worker: the session and the foreground service, system controls, a locked listen with the process unfrozen, and audio focus through a debug-only focus probe. Component tests drive both `Player` implementations, the native-backed one against a fake plugin. The plugin's decision logic lives in plain Java with JVM unit tests. Real earbuds, headphone disconnects, real focus changes and a long locked listen against production stay a manual protocol on the operator's Pixel.

### Every platform

The web player's media session port (built in [#395](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/395), decided in [#388](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/388)) replaces ADR 0011's browser defaults in browsers and iOS. The player host registers `play`, `pause`, `stop` as pause, `seekto`, and `seekbackward` and `seekforward` by the system's offset or 15 s, and no `previoustrack` or `nexttrack`, because there is no queue. It reports the playback state and a position state with the narration track's listed duration, the position including a pending seek, and the playback rate, on load and on every playback change but never on time updates, and clears all of it when the loaded player closes. The system then shows the audiobook's own timeline and skips 15 s, or its own interval, such as iOS's 10 s. On Android the port does nothing, because ExoPlayer's session reports to the system.

## Considered options

- **The WebView's `<audio>` with a mirroring Media3 session** (ADR 0029's first version, [#396](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/396)). Rejected after [#397](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/397): commands from the session get lost once the WebView suspends the hidden page, and every command took a round trip through the page, so the notification's play and pause lagged.
- **A Trusted Web Activity.** Rejected: it runs in whatever browser the listener has, with the same background limits and no native session of Cup's own.
- **Capawesome's Audio Player and Media Session plugins** (compared in the [plugin research](../research/capacitor-media-playback-options.md), 2026-11-14). Rejected: paid and closed source, so their manifests cannot be audited before buying, and they tie Cup to a subscription.
- **`@capgo/capacitor-media-session`** (same [research](../research/capacitor-media-playback-options.md)). Rejected: it lacks `FOREGROUND_SERVICE_MEDIA_PLAYBACK`, handles neither headphone disconnects nor focus, and writes `MPNowPlayingInfoCenter` on iOS.
- **The page saving positions on Android.** Rejected: a suspended page saves nothing, and its saves would race the plugin's ([#425](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/425)).

## Relationship to existing decisions

This supersedes three bullets of [ADR 0011](0011-serve-one-narration-track-and-play-it-with-one-audio-element.md): "One `<audio>` element and no player state machine" and "Native players removed" for Android only, and "System media controls use browser defaults" for every platform. Everything else in ADR 0011 stands. [ADR 0018](0018-test-the-mobile-apps-with-appium-through-webdriverio-on-host-owned-emulators-and-simulators.md) and [ADR 0020](0020-validate-in-three-local-gates-that-trust-the-turbo-cache.md) stay as they are; the validation guides record the current gate steps.

## Consequences

- The track and the position saves no longer depend on the page's JavaScript running, and the `mediaPlayback` foreground service keeps Cup unfrozen and online in the background, as the [foreground service probe](../research/android-foreground-service-playback.md) showed on the emulator on 2026-11-14. So playback, system controls and saves continue with the screen locked or in another app while the service runs.
- Android has a second engine behind the same `Player` port, with its own rules for the end, errors and renewal, which the native-backed player and the plugin must keep in line with the browser's.
- A call longer than the foreground grace may need a manual Play afterwards; that is accepted. Whether an earbud Play after the grace starts ExoPlayer under Android 15's focus rule is checked on the Pixel.
- The Appium suite cannot reproduce the background network cut, because loopback is exempt from it; it shows the unfrozen process that keeps network access. A checked-in manual protocol on the operator's Pixel 10 Pro complements it for earbuds, headphone disconnects, real focus changes and a locked listen of over an hour against production, run as CUP-41's acceptance check and again when the plugin's native code, its Media3 version or the target Android version changes.
- The JVM unit tests make JDK 21 and the Android SDK prerequisites of the review gate, not only of the deploy gate.

### Implementation status

As of 2026-11-16 on the spec's integration branch, [#432](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/432) built the engine: the `createPlayer` seam and the native-backed player, the plugin's commands and `state` events, the session with its buttons, stop as pause, focus and headphone handling, start on the first Play, close from the page, the playback error, the debug-only focus probe, and E2E cases for the session, system controls, a locked listen and focus. It reworked #396's plugin, removed the mirroring player and its command path, and replaced this ADR's first version. [#433](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/433) then built URL expiry and the `403` stop, `closed` reports, adoption of a running session, the JVM tests and the review gate's prerequisites, and [#434](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/434) the native position saves through the signed position URL. The [playback architecture](../architecture/playback.md#in-the-mobile-apps) describes what runs.

On 2026-11-17 [PR #401](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/pull/401) merged the spec's slices to `main` as `c5a5fd8b`.
